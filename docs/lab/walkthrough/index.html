From the record
to the decision.

Brightwater does not exist. Our organisation generator wrote four months of its email, chat and meetings, with every decision, commitment, risk and question planted as a label we can check against. We ran the pieces of the layer on it, one after another. This page shows what each one produced, beside the exact text it came from, including what it missed and what it got wrong.

Everything here is code we have written and are testing, not a finished product. No client’s information is used anywhere. Where a step is not built yet, the page says so and marks it as a thesis. Every number was either measured on this run or is quoted from a component’s held-out benchmark, and says which.

01 / Raw material arrives

157 messages over four months, in three channels.

Brightwater has seven people. Its Germany market entry project sets out to start selling to German customers by the fourth quarter with local data hosting, led by Tariq Nakamura, Head of Operations. Messages run from Tue 6 Jan 2026 to Fri 1 May 2026. org-generator decided every event in code from seed 23; a model then wrote the words, each person keeping one writing habit.

Planted in the timeline, for checking later: 5 decisions, 5 reaffirmations and reversals, 6 commitments, 3 risks, 4 questions, 3 explanations, 3 outcomes and 9 look-alikes: options listed without deciding, doubts and ideas nobody took up. No component sees these labels. Every planted fact was written by the model; none fell back to plain text.

One disclosure. org-generator writes its messages already in the normalizer’s format, so there is no raw export to start from. A script of ours, render.py, writes them back the way tools export them: an mbox with signatures and quoted history, a Slack export without email addresses, WebVTT captions. It adds those wrappers and never changes what a person wrote. Phone numbers in signatures come from the UK range reserved for drama.

This site sets no em dashes. Where a message, or something a component wrote about it, contains one, it is shown on this page as a spaced hyphen (11 places). The run’s own files keep the original text, and nothing else in any quote is changed.

Held-out benchmark, org-generator252 of 253 planted facts were judged present in the text of two held-out organisations. Shown a real Enron email thread beside a generated one, one judge picked the real one 55% of the time (50% means it cannot tell, give or take about 15 points); a second judge picked it 92% of the time.

What went wrong in this run

  • decision-extractor missed 1 of 5 planted decisions.
  • commitment-tracker got 6 due dates wrong; got the status of 3 wrong.
  • risk-finder got the status of 2 wrong.
  • org-generator wrote a fact it had been told to rule out. The plan said annual contracts only, ruling out prices 10% below Ireland; the message it wrote says “Pricing goes 10% below Ireland for year one.” decision-extractor and the second brief then report that as decided. They read the text correctly; the text was wrong.
  • commitment-tracker resolves “by Friday 13 February” to the Friday after the message, not to 13 February. All six due dates are wrong, and the wrong dates appear in both briefs.
  • decision-extractor, commitment-tracker, risk-finder read one conversation at a time, so a change made in another conversation (a reversal, a promise kept, a risk that came true) is not joined to what it changes. Neither reversal was linked to the decision it undid.
  • decision-extractor gave the 2 March hosting reversal only the status “reversed”. Its statement names Dublin, but it recorded no decision made to keep everything there, so the decision this walkthrough follows is not among its decisions made. For the other reversal it recorded both.
  • commitment-tracker, inside the second brief marked the employer-of-record shortlist “cancelled”. It slipped from 6 April to 27 April and was never delivered: overdue, not cancelled.
  • redactor took three message ids for email addresses. Harmless here, but a false positive all the same.
  • contradiction-detector gave different answers on the same record. Run on its own, it reported the Frankfurt conflict; run again inside the second brief, it reported only the changes of plan, so the second brief has no conflicts section.
  • normalizer moved the sign-off name (“Mateo”, “Sven”) into the signature in 13 of 43 emails. No sentence was lost, but the body is not exactly what the person wrote.

What the labels cannot check

Items a finder reported that no planted label covers. These are not counted as errors: some are true and simply unlabelled, some are not. Each is listed, with its quote, in its finder’s panel.

  • decision-extractor 16 items, citing the kickoff, commitments, answers, work reported done, explanations, small talk and status, slipped dates and pointers to who knows.
  • commitment-tracker 1 item, citing small talk and status.
  • risk-finder 9 items, citing explanations, reversals, slipped dates, lessons and results.
  • gap-finder 19 items, citing small talk and status, risks, options listed without deciding, decisions, doubts, live issues, ideas nobody took up, pointers to who knows, explanations, reversals and results.
  • contradiction-detector 1 item.
  • expertise-finder 15 items, citing risks, options listed without deciding, decisions, answers, reaffirmed decisions, pointers to who knows, reversals and results.

What is not built

  • Documents. org-generator writes email, chat and meetings only, so nothing here was read from a document.
  • The layer as a system: a store the company owns, with permissions enforced on who and what may read it.
  • Keeping an outcome against the decision it judges, so the next decision starts from it.
  • A brief framed around one pending decision; brief-writer briefs everything it is given.
  • The control point in the path: redactor and egrysa are not wired between the finders and the model.
  • A test of identity-resolver on hard cases here: every source showed full names.
  • Raw exports from real tools: the ones read here were rendered by us from the generator’s messages.
